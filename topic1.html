Topic 1:

How User Generated Content is used as a tool on the Web

User Generated Content as a Developmental Tool

User Generated Content can be used to iterate and develop the very tools its hosted on! Tech interested users on the web will often post free software tools or new development projects on a set website or database. In this way, the content is used as a unique aid for other users on the web.

While development content is usually managed by major companies, individual users still make their own content. This is often referred to as “open source” or “open software” due to UGC’s public and open nature. This type of of UGC is often used to benefit other users in their own developments or own usages of internet software.

If you have every used a unofficial mod or extension for a product, it was likely a development tool posted to the web by another user! Certain high level coding languages or conversion programs were made and distributed by users too. Certain forms of development UGC can also be released by dedicated users to aid companies in debugging, cybersecurity, and other upkeep measures.

Examples

GitHub

SourceForge

User Generated Content as a Informational Tool

This was one of the first forms of User Generated Content on the internet. Humanity for centuries have passed down and shared knowledge amongst each other in order to better themselves. User Generated Content on the internet opened a entirely new avenue of near-immediate access to a impossibly large collection of data.

Whether it be videos, images, or simple text on a screen, one of the most prominent ways to share information in the time periods of the web has been through UGC. Most people born into this era, with ample access to technology, have seen this in action. Users have generated many games, tutorials, interactive websites, or even simple databases that compile information specifically designed to educate and inform. It is so prominent, in fact, that the phrasing “Google It!” is synonymous with learning and checking information quickly through the open web.

An entire area of design study can now be allocated to things like “infographics”, which are quick, image based UGC types that convey information in a unobtrusive and compact way. Most of humanity's greatest achievements can be taught, or atleast simplified educationally, through the natures of educated users creating information content to share.

Examples

Wikipedia

OpenStax

User Generated Content as a Entertainment Tool

As a Web 2.0 user, this is likely one of the most identifiable examples of User Generated Content in your everyday life. The term “content creator” is literally coined because of a specific user’s role in creating content.

Entertainment is also a concept that has existed for hundreds of years, and is now adapting to the growing technologies of the world. Textual entertainment used to take the form of books or general papers. Visual entertainment used to take the form of live theatre, watching physical activity, or television. Audio entertainment used to be simplistic music, live music, or radio. Even sensual entertainment could be described by physical exercise (sports) or early video games. In a way, these are all “user” generated forms of content, provided you view a individual as a “user” of space itself.

Now, the web has allowed for entire entertainment roles in society to be filled and spanned across entire continents. The interconnected web and allowance of user contribution has naturally brough entertainment into its sphere. Thinking about the transference of non-contributive entertainment to the contributive concept we share now, you can almost see a shift in the interactions humans have taken. This shift being further away from experiences in the real world, and more into creating experiences in the cyber one.

Examples

Youtube

Twitch

User Generated Content as a Social Tool

This, alongside entertainment, is another one of the most common things that users have come to mind when talking about User Generated Content. Social interactions on the internet are almost entirely user generated. There is very little corporate or market influence in social interaction.

Social media, forums, text chats, calls, posts, and more. Just about any interaction between another user on the internet involves you generating some sort of content, and receiving generated content back. It one of the most important staples of the world wide web.

Social content can also be in the context of working individuals using the web as a tool to conduct peer-to-peer communication. Even if they are not necessarily producing public goods or open content, they are still contributing in the participatory web on a micro scale.

User Generated Content as a Anti-Development Tool

While the circumstances around User Generated Content being used in this way can very easily cross into misusage territory, the possible implementations of open anti-development tools and work on the web are listed in a different section for a reason.

Anti-development, for lack of a better word, is basically a program or series of work done in order to hurt, hinder, or otherwise hack another program or user. This is a obvious breach of internet legality and privacy if done incorrectly. However, “White Hat” hackers and hacking groups are established specifically to benefit the open web with safe, legal usages of their skill sets.

Kali Linux, Burp, and Metasploit are a few examples of user generated content intended for pentesting and cybersecurity analysis. They are used to aid individuals in a working sense, allowing for reverse development and breaking down of web products. At the end of it all, these tools are still likely intended to be positive contributions to the web. Misuse can and will always occur, but the original UGC should still be recognized as a legitimately designed tool for the open web.
