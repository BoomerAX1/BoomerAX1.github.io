Topic 2:

Issues and Misuse with User Generated Content

Social Issues and Misuse

Due to the open nature of the free internet, and the oftentimes loose fit rules on what can and cannot be made, there are multiple instances of social issues and instability amongst peers regarding the content that users generate.

Moral

As long as the content abides by regional and general law (more on law issues in coming slides), User Generated Content can be nearly anything. There are many social moral issues between worldwide users on the web regarding content that may have very negative cultural or regional connotations. For example, Pornographic or Gore content is very shunned in certain cultures and environments, but is neutral ground for the internet provided that all laws and regulations are followed. Strife may arise between users and activism groups on limitation of these types of content.

Political

Politics, no matter where you live, is usually a point of contention. While early forms of technological interconnectivity enabled easier access to political information, the User Generated Content of the Web 2.0 era has allowed for users to make their own opinions and political messages very public and open. Issues now arise with users creating content deemed offensive, controversial, retaliatory, or otherwise oppository to the political opinions of others. Many users have problems when political issues or opinions enter the stream of content presented on the interconnected internet.

The following examples are some slight variations of the general Political and Moral Social Issues that UGC can present.

Infighting

Web users often will become hostile to each other (often in the same sub-communities) due to differing opinions or types of content. As a example, these are common problems that occur in writing or story-oriented communities, when certain users generate content that other users do not approve of (fanfiction, non-canon stories, rewrites, etc.)

Trolling

Some users on the internet use UGC exclusively for the act of “trolling”, or purposefully doing things to upset others. This can span many mediums (such as purposeful lying, troll imagery or “copypastas” to annoy people), but in of itself is still considered content.

Expectations

Certain web users or content consumers can take issues amongst others based off false expectations. Someone who creates review videos, for example, could take issues with an online content creator (such as a game developer or mod developer) for not releasing quick or quality material, despite it never being promised. This is a extension of infighting, but often reveals itself through UGC rather than a simple dispute (IE. angry reddit posts, twitter posts, forum posts).

Vocal Minorities

Vocal minorities (or majorities) can flood the open web with UGC pertaining to their specific topic. Other users may get irritated constantly seeing material from these users, often as a extension to social reaction to political issues or opinions.

Legal Issues and Misuse

General social discourse is much more different online than legal issues. As aforementioned, the web has very loose rules on User Generated Content depending on where you find yourself. However, generally accepted laws, as well as regional laws apply to almost any human interaction (internet, or not!)

Regional and Federal Law

Most regions and federal systems have general laws and regulations for their citizens, but have also had to modify or adapt them to interactions that happen over the internet. This is where most of the regulation to web content comes from. While the internet is technically open to absolutely any content, 99% of the legally connected web follow regulations that are put in place to limit the absolute worst types of humanity possible. Child Pornography, for example, is one of the most banned and prosecuted User Generated Content types in the world. Content like gore, normal pornography, or other unsavory things may be banned in a large chunk of countries, with the countries that allow it facing social scrutiny discussed in the previous slides.

International Law

While less noticeable than regional or federal laws to the average user, internet interactions and content are often monitored by many security professionals across the world for actions like terrorism, arms smuggling, drug trades and trafficking, assasination, and other conspiratorial international crimes. User Generated Content exchanged or displayed over video games such as Roblox or Minecraft are historic examples of persecution under this type of interaction.

The following legal issues are sometimes less severe, or simply non-existent depending on the country. However, they are still persecuted depending on the medium of User Generated Content.

Copyright Law

To prevent misappropriation of user or market content, many countries have specific copyright laws in place that require a user to conduct certain procedures to distribute and user another user’s content. For example, a web user in the US needs to go through proper procedures to upload a edit of another user or companies film production. If they do so without proper permissions or procedures, they could be arrested or have to pay a large fine alongside removal of their altered content. Some countries don’t implement this, but may be subject to user cross-investigation.

Cross-Investigation

If content from a country that is looser on certain laws or regulations is interfering with the interactions of a more strict country, cross-investigation and removal could occur or certain websites and applications could be blocked from the strict country in question. This can harm the website or application, causing them to try to abide by multiple international rules in order to retain traffic.

Privacy and Data

Similar to copyright laws, but on a more personal scale. Many countries have laws and regulations in place in order to protect the identities, information, and data privacy of a individual. However, there are just as many practices in different businesses and governments that attempt to circumnavigate this concept in order to collect from users on the web.
